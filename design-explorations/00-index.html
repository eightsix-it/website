Eight Six IT Engineering · Design exploration · September 2026

Ten answers to one question

What could eightsixit.com look like if it stopped being designed like every other AI-generated SaaS site? Ten competing directions, each a working page with the real copy, so the identity can be judged rather than imagined.

None of these touch production. Each is a self-contained page: its own typography, layout logic, palette and interaction model. Six of them reinterpret the wordmark typographically; four keep the current logo. The point is not consensus — pick the one or two worth evolving, and say what you like and dislike about the rest.

  1. 01

    Field Notes

    EDITORIAL · WARM PAPER · SINGLE COLUMN · MARGINALIA

    The site as a letter from the engineer, in chapters. A founder-led firm has one asset no franchise MSP can copy: a person with a point of view. So the page reads like one, with sidenotes and footnotes carrying the specifications so the prose stays human. Newsreader for text, Plex Mono for apparatus. The quietest direction here and probably the most trustworthy.

    Nav chapter list, sticky, tracks readingRisk long; rewards readers, loses skimmersOpen 01
  2. 02

    Runbook

    OPERATIONS MANUAL · NUMBERED SECTIONS · TABLES · CALLOUTS

    Published in the format the firm actually delivers work in: a controlled document with a revision block, numbered clauses, a service matrix, procedures, NOTE/CAUTION/WARNING callouts and a signature page. It says “we are engineers who write things down” without a single adjective. Dense by design. Source Sans and Source Code Pro.

    Nav sticky contents index, escalation boxRisk intimidating to a non-technical buyerOpen 02
  3. 03

    Console

    TERMINAL SESSION · MONOSPACE · KEYBOARD-DRIVEN · DARK

    The whole page is a shell session: commands and their output, a man page for the FAQ, an assessment request answered at a prompt. Keys 1–7 jump, slash opens a search palette. No fake dashboard floating in space; the page itself is the instrument. One accent (the brand yellow), no glow, no gradients. JetBrains Mono throughout.

    Nav numbered keys + command paletteRisk reads “for developers” to a clinic managerOpen 03
  4. 04

    Swiss

    INTERNATIONAL TYPOGRAPHIC STYLE · 12-COLUMN GRID · BLACK / WHITE / RED

    Rigor as identity. A visible modular grid (press G), flush-left grotesk, hairline rules, one red accent per spread, zero radius, zero shadow. The sectors become a real table; the process becomes four columns. Confidence through restraint rather than through motion. Archivo, set tight.

    Nav single line, right-aligned, red CTA underlineRisk can read as cold; drops the navy entirelyOpen 04
  5. 05

    Dos Idiomas

    BILINGUAL PARALLEL TEXT · CIVIC DOCUMENT · CREAM / NAVY / RED

    The market is Puerto Rico, and every clinic administrator and law-office manager reads Spanish first. So the page is bilingual by construction: Spanish and English side by side, paragraph for paragraph, like an official notice or a contract. A toggle sets which language leads. The grid is the idea. Bitter slab for headings, Karla for text.

    Nav language switch is the primary controlRisk doubles every word you ever writeOpen 05
  6. 06

    Blueprint

    ENGINEERING DRAWING · SCHEMATIC · TITLE BLOCK · CALLOUTS

    The homepage is drawing ES-100: a line schematic of a typical managed environment, numbered callouts tied to general notes, dimension lines for RTO and RPO, and a title block signed by the engineer. Sheets 200–500 carry the schedule, site conditions, sequence and notes to owner. Hover a callout and the part lights up. Flat blueprint blue, no glow. Saira and Roboto Mono.

    Nav sheet index in the headerRisk the metaphor is strong; it has to be kept up on every pageOpen 06
  7. 07

    Work Order

    FIELD SERVICE · HI-VIS · CONDENSED TYPE · FORMS

    A contractor, not a SaaS. Safety yellow and black, hazard stripes, a giant phone number, and the page laid out as the work order you would sign: reported problem (tick the boxes), scope of work, parts on the truck, how the job runs, terms with a NO LOCK-IN stamp. Physical, honest, unmistakably local. Barlow Condensed.

    Nav none needed; the form is the pageRisk louder than the AI practice may want to beOpen 07
  8. 08

    Isla

    CARTOGRAPHIC · SERVICE-AREA MAP · GARAMOND · GAZETTEER

    Rooted in place. A drawn map of Puerto Rico with San Juan as home base, a graticule, compass and scale bar; then a gazetteer of services, a field guide to the sectors, a route from first call to fully managed. No fake client pins, just an honest service area: the whole island on site, remote everywhere. EB Garamond and Alegreya Sans.

    Nav small caps, classicRisk “local” can read as “small” to a plant GMOpen 08
  9. 09

    Plain Text

    DELIBERATELY UNSTYLED · SYSTEM SERIF · TABLES · ONE REQUEST

    The anti-design control. Black text, blue links, bordered tables, native disclosure widgets, no webfonts, no scripts, no images. It loads in one request and says so. This is what confidence looks like when a firm would rather spend the budget on engineering; it is also a fair baseline to measure the other nine against.

    Nav a numbered contents listRisk polarizing; some buyers read plain as cheapOpen 09
  10. 10

    Chart

    CLINICAL RECORD · S / O / A / P · TABS · TABULAR DATA

    Healthcare is where the firm cut its teeth, and it built a SOAP-note scribe. So the homepage is a chart: Subjective (what practices tell us), Objective (the vitals we measure, with targets), Assessment (problem list with a plan per problem), Plan (the first thirty days, ending in “Order: free assessment”). Tabs, tabular numerals, one red for critical flags. Public Sans.

    Nav chart tabs, arrow-key navigableRisk healthcare-coded; law and plant buyers may feel like visitorsOpen 10
